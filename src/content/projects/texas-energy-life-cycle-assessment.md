problem: |
  Most comparisons look at one thing and pick a winner. Environmental studies usually favor solar. Cost studies usually favor gas. The impact on workers and communities barely comes up.

  I wanted all three side by side, measured on the same 1 MWh, so the tradeoffs are out in the open.
approach: |
  I compared a Texas natural gas plant against a solar plus battery system. For the environment, I tracked emissions, water, and land from extraction to end of life. For cost, I ran a life cycle cost analysis and included the cost of outages, using ERCOT's value of lost load of about $35,000 per MWh. For people, I looked at jobs and health impacts on the communities nearby.
resultNote: |
  Nothing wins everywhere. Gas is cheaper over its life, and the gap gets bigger once you count outages. It also supports more jobs, about 15 to 20 per MW. Solar plus battery is much cleaner to run.

  The biggest emissions hotspot on the solar side is lithium mining, before the plant is ever built. On the gas side it's burning the gas, and nothing upstream fixes that.
limits: |
  You can't add the three scores together. How much each one matters is a judgment call, and I leave that to the reader. The outage cost swings the cost results a lot, and social impact is the hardest of the three to measure, so treat it as the roughest of the three.
