problem: |
  Generation comparisons usually pick one pillar and declare a winner. Environmental studies favor solar, cost studies often favor gas, and social impacts rarely enter the comparison at all.

  Running all three pillars on the same functional unit makes the trade visible instead of letting the choice of pillar decide the answer in advance.
approach: |
  I assessed a Texas natural gas plant against a solar-plus-battery system on a functional unit of 1 MWh, across three pillars.

  Environmental LCA covers the emissions inventory from extraction through combustion or manufacture. Life cycle costing includes value of lost load, using the ERCOT figure of roughly $35,000 per MWh, so reliability shows up as a cost rather than as a footnote. Social LCA covers the labor and community impacts along each supply chain.
resultNote: |
  No system wins on all three pillars. Each one leads somewhere and trails somewhere else, so the comparison ends in a trade rather than a ranking.

  The hotspots are specific. On the solar-plus-storage side the single biggest emissions contributor is lithium extraction, upstream of the plant and outside the operator's control. On the gas side it is combustion itself, which no amount of upstream improvement removes.
limits: |
  Three-pillar results cannot be summed. Weighting the pillars against each other is a value judgment, and the study does not make that judgment for the reader.

  The value of lost load figure drives the cost pillar hard. ERCOT's roughly $35,000 per MWh is a system-level administrative number, and moving it moves the cost conclusion. Social LCA is the least standardized of the three pillars, so its results carry wider uncertainty than the emissions inventory.
